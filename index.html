The consultation

What actually happens when you come in

Consultations are booked as assessments, not as treatments. Nothing is administered on a first visit without one, and we will decline to treat where it is not clinically appropriate.

Request a consultation
01
History and examination

Medical history, medications, previous procedures, and an examination of the area you have asked about. Around 30 to 45 minutes.

02
Options, risks and cost

What is suitable, what it involves, what the risks and downtime are, and what it costs in full. Including the option of doing nothing.

03
Time to decide, then consent

You leave with written information and no pressure to book. Any procedure is consented separately, on a later appointment.

04
Review and aftercare

A scheduled review, written aftercare, and a number to call if something concerns you. You can also raise a concern directly with AHPRA at any time.

Things a clinic should tell you before you book

Ask us anything before you book →

Outcomes vary between individuals. What one person experiences after a procedure does not predict what you will experience. Anyone who promises you a specific result is guessing.

Every procedure carries risk. Bruising, swelling and tenderness are common. Less common complications exist and are described in full on each procedure's page, along with what to do if one occurs.

Most effects are temporary. Maintaining a result means repeat appointments and ongoing cost. We will tell you the likely frequency before you start, not after.

Some medicines cannot be advertised. Australian law restricts what a clinic may publish about prescription-only treatments. That is why this site describes consultations rather than products. Details are discussed in person, with a practitioner.

You can complain. If you are unhappy with your care, tell us first, and know that you may also raise a concern with AHPRA regardless of any agreement you have signed.
